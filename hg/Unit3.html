Unit 3: Cultural Patterns and Processes

Culture, Language, Religion, Diffusion, and Cultural Landscapes  |  Exam Weight: 12–17%

🗣️ Unit Overview

AP Human Geography Unit 3 focuses on how culture shapes the world and how cultural traits spread across space. Culture includes language, religion, food, clothing, music, architecture, customs, values, and everyday ways of life.

This unit is important because culture affects identity, landscapes, politics, migration, conflict, globalization, and how people connect to places. You will study folk culture, popular culture, cultural landscapes, language families, religions, diffusion, assimilation, acculturation, syncretism, and multiculturalism.

Big question: How do cultural practices, languages, religions, and identities spread and shape places over time?

Topic 3.1 — Introduction to Culture

🌍 What Is Culture?

Culture is the shared way of life of a group of people. It includes both visible traits, like clothing and architecture, and less visible traits, like beliefs, values, language, traditions, and social rules.

Main Parts of Culture

  • Cultural traits: Individual parts of culture, such as food, clothing, music, language, or holidays.
  • Cultural complex: A group of related cultural traits, such as baseball including uniforms, rules, stadiums, fans, and traditions.
  • Cultural hearth: A place where a cultural trait or culture begins and spreads from.
  • Customs: Common practices followed by a group.
  • Taboos: Behaviors strongly forbidden by a culture.

Folk Culture vs. Popular Culture

  • Folk culture: Traditional practices of small, usually rural groups. It spreads slowly and is often tied to local environment.
  • Popular culture: Widespread culture found across large societies. It spreads quickly through media, technology, and globalization.
  • Example of folk culture: Traditional clothing, local music, handmade crafts, or regional food customs.
  • Example of popular culture: Fast food chains, global music trends, sports brands, movies, and social media trends.

Why Culture Matters in Geography

Culture changes landscapes and creates regional differences. For example, religion can shape sacred sites, language can shape place names, and food customs can shape agriculture and trade.

Topic 3.2 — Cultural Landscapes

🏘️ Culture Written on the Land

A cultural landscape is the visible human imprint on the physical environment. Buildings, roads, farms, religious structures, signs, monuments, neighborhoods, and land-use patterns can all show culture.

Examples of Cultural Landscapes

  • Religious landscapes: Churches, mosques, temples, cemeteries, shrines, and sacred spaces.
  • Linguistic landscapes: Street signs, business names, advertisements, and place names in different languages.
  • Agricultural landscapes: Terraced farms, rice paddies, vineyards, barns, irrigation systems, and crop patterns.
  • Urban landscapes: Architecture, neighborhoods, public spaces, transportation systems, and ethnic enclaves.
  • Commercial landscapes: Shopping centers, fast food restaurants, hotels, billboards, and corporate chains.

Sequent Occupance

Sequent occupance is the idea that different groups leave layers of cultural evidence on the landscape over time. A city may show Indigenous place names, colonial buildings, immigrant neighborhoods, modern skyscrapers, and global brands all in one place.

Why Cultural Landscapes Matter

  • They show who lived in a place and how they used it.
  • They reveal power, identity, religion, language, wealth, and inequality.
  • They can preserve culture, but they can also show cultural change or conflict.

Topic 3.3 — Cultural Patterns

📍 Where Culture Appears

Cultural patterns are the way cultural traits are arranged across space. Geographers look for where cultures are clustered, where boundaries exist, and how cultural traits connect to physical and human geography.

How Cultural Patterns Form

  • Environment: Climate, landforms, and resources influence food, clothing, housing, and agriculture.
  • History: Migration, conquest, trade, and colonization shape culture.
  • Religion: Religious beliefs can shape diet, architecture, holidays, and sacred spaces.
  • Language: Language creates cultural regions and influences identity.
  • Economics: Wealth, technology, and globalization spread popular culture.

Identity and Culture

  • Ethnicity: Shared cultural heritage, such as language, ancestry, religion, or traditions.
  • Nationality: Legal identity connected to a state or country.
  • Gender roles: Social expectations tied to gender that vary across cultures.
  • Ethnocentrism: Judging another culture by the standards of one’s own culture.
  • Cultural relativism: Understanding a culture on its own terms instead of judging it as inferior.

AP Exam Tip

If a question asks about cultural patterns, do not just define the term. Describe where the pattern appears, why it appears there, and how it affects people or landscapes.

Topic 3.4 — Types of Diffusion

🌊 How Culture Spreads

Diffusion is the spread of people, ideas, technology, diseases, religions, languages, or cultural practices from one place to another. AP Human Geography questions often ask you to identify the type of diffusion shown in a situation.

Relocation Diffusion

  • Culture spreads because people physically move.
  • Example: Migrants bring their language, religion, food, or holidays to a new country.
  • This is very common with migration, religion, and language.

Expansion Diffusion

  • Culture spreads outward from a hearth while staying strong in the original place.
  • Expansion diffusion has three main types: contagious, hierarchical, and stimulus diffusion.

Contagious Diffusion

  • Spreads rapidly from person to person, like a wave.
  • Example: Viral social media trends, slang, memes, or diseases.

Hierarchical Diffusion

  • Spreads from important people or major places to smaller places.
  • Example: Fashion trends spreading from global cities like Paris, New York, or Tokyo to smaller cities.

Stimulus Diffusion

  • The main idea spreads, but people change it to fit local culture.
  • Example: A global fast food company changing menu items to match local tastes.

Topic 3.5 — Historical Causes of Diffusion

⛵ Trade, Migration, Conquest, and Colonialism

Cultural diffusion has happened throughout history. Before the internet and modern transportation, culture still spread through trade, migration, conquest, religious missions, empires, and colonialism.

Historical Forces That Spread Culture

  • Trade routes: The Silk Roads, Indian Ocean trade, and trans-Saharan routes spread goods, languages, religions, and ideas.
  • Migration: Moving people brought languages, foodways, religions, and customs into new regions.
  • Conquest: Empires spread languages, laws, religions, architecture, and political systems.
  • Colonialism: European powers spread Christianity, European languages, plantation systems, and new borders.
  • Missionaries: Religious workers spread faiths, schools, writing systems, and cultural values.

Colonialism and Culture

Colonialism had major cultural effects. In many places, European languages became official languages. Christianity spread widely. Colonial borders sometimes divided ethnic groups or forced different groups into one state. These cultural effects still matter today.

Topic 3.6 — Contemporary Causes of Diffusion

📱 Globalization and Modern Media

Today, culture spreads faster than ever because of transportation, the internet, social media, streaming platforms, tourism, migration, multinational corporations, and global trade.

Modern Forces That Spread Culture

  • Social media: Trends, music, fashion, slang, and political ideas spread quickly across borders.
  • Television and film: Movies, shows, and celebrities spread popular culture globally.
  • Tourism: Tourists bring cultural expectations and influence local economies and landscapes.
  • Migration: Migrants carry language, religion, food, and traditions into new places.
  • Multinational corporations: Global brands spread products, logos, advertisements, and lifestyles.

Cultural Convergence and Divergence

  • Cultural convergence: Cultures become more similar because of globalization.
  • Cultural divergence: Cultures become more different or protect themselves from outside influence.
  • Example of convergence: People around the world wearing similar brands or using the same apps.
  • Example of divergence: A group preserves a local language or tradition to resist cultural change.

Topic 3.7 — Diffusion of Religion and Language

🗣️ Language Patterns

Language is one of the strongest markers of culture. It shapes identity, communication, education, politics, and place names. Languages spread through migration, conquest, colonization, trade, and globalization.

Language Concepts

  • Language family: A group of languages with a shared ancient origin.
  • Indo-European language family: A major language family that includes English, Spanish, French, Hindi, Russian, and many others.
  • Dialect: A regional version of a language with differences in pronunciation, vocabulary, or grammar.
  • Lingua franca: A common language used by people who speak different native languages.
  • Pidgin language: A simplified language that develops for communication between groups.
  • Creole language: A stable language formed from mixing languages, often in colonial or trade settings.
  • Toponym: A place name that can reveal culture, history, language, or power.

Language and Power

Language can create unity, but it can also create conflict. Governments may promote one official language to unify a country, while minority groups may fight to preserve their languages as part of their identity.

🕌 Religion Patterns

Religion shapes culture, identity, architecture, holidays, food rules, clothing, sacred spaces, migration, conflict, and political boundaries. Different religions spread in different ways.

Universalizing Religions

  • Universalizing religions actively seek converts and are not limited to one ethnic group.
  • Christianity: Spread through missionaries, migration, colonialism, and relocation diffusion.
  • Islam: Spread through trade, conquest, missionaries, migration, and cultural contact.
  • Buddhism: Spread from South Asia into East and Southeast Asia through missionaries, trade, and cultural contact.

Ethnic Religions

  • Ethnic religions are closely tied to one cultural group or place.
  • Hinduism: Strongly concentrated in India and Nepal.
  • Judaism: Closely tied to Jewish ethnic identity and history, though Jewish communities exist worldwide.
  • Shinto: Strongly associated with Japanese culture and identity.

Sacred Spaces

  • Sacred site: A place considered holy or spiritually important.
  • Pilgrimage: Travel to a sacred place for religious reasons.
  • Religious architecture: Churches, mosques, temples, synagogues, shrines, and stupas shape cultural landscapes.

Topic 3.8 — Effects of Diffusion

🌐 What Happens When Cultures Mix?

Cultural diffusion can create new cultural forms, but it can also create tension. When cultures interact, people may adopt, blend, resist, or transform cultural traits.

Important Effects

  • Acculturation: One group adopts some traits of another culture while still keeping parts of its original culture.
  • Assimilation: One group loses much of its original culture and becomes more like the dominant culture.
  • Syncretism: Different cultural or religious traditions blend into a new form.
  • Multiculturalism: Multiple cultures exist in the same society and are recognized or valued.
  • Globalization: Increasing connections among places through trade, media, migration, and technology.

Positive Effects of Diffusion

  • New foods, music, languages, religions, and ideas spread.
  • People can learn from other cultures and form new cultural combinations.
  • Economic opportunities can grow through tourism, trade, and global markets.

Negative Effects of Diffusion

  • Local cultures may weaken because of global popular culture.
  • Indigenous languages and traditions may decline.
  • Cultural conflict can occur when groups feel threatened or excluded.
  • Commodification can turn cultural traditions into products for tourists or profit.

📝 Multiple Choice Practice

Click an answer to check it. Each question includes an explanation.

1. Which example best shows relocation diffusion?

(A) A fashion trend spreads from Paris to smaller cities.
(B) Migrants bring their language and religion to a new country.
(C) A meme spreads rapidly through social media.
(D) A restaurant changes its menu to fit local tastes.
Answer: B. Relocation diffusion happens when people physically move and bring cultural traits with them.

2. A global fast food chain changing its menu to match local tastes is an example of which type of diffusion?

(A) Contagious diffusion
(B) Relocation diffusion
(C) Stimulus diffusion
(D) Reverse diffusion
Answer: C. Stimulus diffusion means the main idea spreads, but people change it to fit local culture.

3. Which feature is most clearly part of a cultural landscape?

(A) Plate tectonics below Earth’s surface
(B) Ocean currents in the Pacific
(C) A mosque, church, or temple in a city
(D) Wind patterns over a desert
Answer: C. Cultural landscapes are visible human imprints, such as religious buildings, signs, roads, farms, and neighborhoods.

4. Which religion is usually classified as an ethnic religion?

(A) Christianity
(B) Islam
(C) Buddhism
(D) Hinduism
Answer: D. Hinduism is strongly tied to India and is usually classified as an ethnic religion.

5. Which term means adopting some traits of another culture while still keeping parts of the original culture?

(A) Acculturation
(B) Assimilation
(C) Distance decay
(D) Sovereignty
Answer: A. Acculturation means adopting some traits from another culture while still keeping parts of the original culture.

⭐ Key Takeaways

  • Culture is a shared way of life. It includes language, religion, food, clothing, customs, values, and identity.
  • Cultural landscapes show culture visually. Buildings, signs, farms, sacred sites, and neighborhoods reveal cultural patterns.
  • Folk culture and popular culture spread differently. Folk culture is local and slower; popular culture is widespread and faster.
  • Diffusion is central to this unit. Know relocation, contagious, hierarchical, and stimulus diffusion.
  • History shaped culture. Trade, migration, conquest, colonialism, and religion spread cultural traits across regions.
  • Language creates identity and regions. Language families, dialects, lingua francas, and toponyms all show cultural geography.
  • Religion shapes landscapes and movement. Sacred sites, pilgrimages, architecture, and diffusion patterns are major AP topics.
  • Globalization creates both convergence and resistance. Some cultures become more similar, while others work to preserve local identity.
